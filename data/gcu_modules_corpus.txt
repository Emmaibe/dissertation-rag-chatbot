Key Facts: The Advanced Programming module (MMI330759) is worth 15 credits at SCQF Level 11 in the Computing subject area.

Module Description:
Summary of content

This module assumes that students already have experience on, at least, one object-oriented programming language. It aims to help students becoming better and more versatile programmers. Students will be equipped with a broad understanding of the nature of a range of programming paradigms, and the support provided for each paradigm in currently popular programming languages, enabling to make appropriate choices to best meet the requirements of a specific programming problem. MODULE: Advanced Topics in Human Computer Interaction
Module Code: MMG527109
SCQF Level: 11
Credits: 15 credits
Subject Area: Applied Computer Games
School: School of Science & Engineering, Glasgow Caledonian University
URL: https://www.gcu.ac.uk/currentstudents/essentials/modules/module-detail?module=MMG527109

Key Facts: The Advanced Topics in Human Computer Interaction module (MMG527109) is worth 15 credits at SCQF Level 11 in the Applied Computer Games subject area.

Module Description:
Summary of content

Human Computer Interaction (HCI) broadens out to encompass more of how users experience computing systems (UX), and how they may change the way people work. This includes the ease of use and enjoyability of the product, and affective reactions more generally, including for games and other enjoyable technologies. This advanced module builds on the earlier foundational module for HCI, to the point where students can design and conduct their own user studies for various types of applications and computing devices in recently developing technologies.
Wider notions of affect will be covered, including the centrality of human emotion and personality to the user experience, for games and other entertainment devices, for social media platforms, and for the potential uses of technologies to support negotiation and problem solving between humans and even artificial agents. Students should thus be better prepared to consider more career options, as they will be able to contribute to the analysis as well as design of a wide range of industrial products and services.

MODULE: Cyber Defence and Penetration Testing
Module Code: MMI125226
SCQF Level: 11
Credits: 15 credits
Subject Area: Computer Networks & Security
School: School of Science & Engineering, Glasgow Caledonian University
URL: https://www.gcu.ac.uk/currentstudents/essentials/modules/module-detail?module=MMI125226

Key Facts: The Cyber Defence and Penetration Testing module (MMI125226) is worth 15 credits at SCQF Level 11 in the Computer Networks & Security subject area.

Module Description:
Summary of content

This module focuses on teaching the methods and techniques used in penetration testing. This is the practice of legally and ethically
assessing the security of systems and networks by identifying possible weaknesses. The overall aim of penetration testing is strengthening defences through the means of attacks. The aim of this module is to appreciate the various stages of a penetration test, while analysing how ethical hackers can use tools to break into insecure network systems and how these attacks may be mitigated. The module examines hacking techniques used to break into networked systems, and explores the usage of malware in the context of a hacking attack.

MODULE: Cyber Forensics and Incident Response
Module Code: MMI125225
SCQF Level: 11
Credits: 15 credits
Subject Area: Computer Networks & Security
School: School of Science & Engineering, Glasgow Caledonian University
URL: https://www.gcu.ac.uk/currentstudents/essentials/modules/module-detail?module=MMI125225

Key Facts: The Cyber Forensics and Incident Response module (MMI125225) is worth 15 credits at SCQF Level 11 in the Computer Networks & Security subject area.

Module Description:
Summary of content

The prevalence of cyber security incidents highlights the increase of potential risks in the digital arena. These vulnerabilities are fuelling the growing need for skilled professionals who can protect systems from attack, respond to security breaches and solve cybercrimes. This module builds on an understanding of the main principles behind digital forensics, together with the procedures for securing and analysing evidence to support investigations. It introduces the construction and development of different types of malicious code, including their implementation and methods of action in exploiting system weaknesses.
The module focuses on the development of crucial analytical techniques for responding to security breaches and investigating cyber incidents. This includes the analysis, evaluation and reverse engineering of malicious code for forensic examination as well as the development of strategies useful for defending and recovering from attacks. The ethical and professional issues/requirements of the professional practitioner are incorporated throughout the syllabus.

Summary of content

This course aims to equip students with knowledge and skills required for successful data visualisation.
